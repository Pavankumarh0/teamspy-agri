# Tomato Leaf Problems

Review status: starter content, to be checked against the official package of practices.

## Why tomato leaves turn yellow
Yellow leaves on tomato have several possible causes, and the pattern of yellowing helps tell them apart. Nitrogen deficiency makes the older, lower leaves turn evenly pale yellow first, while new leaves stay green. Magnesium deficiency makes older leaves turn yellow between the veins while the veins stay green. Overwatering or waterlogging damages the roots, so leaves turn yellow and plants wilt even though the soil is wet. Early blight, a fungal disease, starts as brown spots with rings like a target on lower leaves, with yellow tissue around the spots. Fusarium wilt makes leaves yellow and wilt, often on one side of the plant first, and the inside of the stem turns brown. Tomato leaf curl virus makes leaves small, curled upward and yellowish, and plants stunted; it is spread by whiteflies. It is also normal for the oldest leaves at the very bottom to yellow slowly as the plant ages.

## What to do about yellow leaves
First check the watering: if the soil is soggy, water less and improve drainage. If older leaves are evenly yellow and the plant is growing slowly, nitrogen may be short; follow your soil test recommendation for nitrogen. Remove and destroy leaves with blight spots, avoid wetting the leaves, and give space between plants for air flow, because blight spreads in wet, crowded conditions. Pull out and destroy plants with leaf curl virus or wilt so they do not spread to healthy plants, and control whiteflies. Rotate tomato with crops from a different family, because wilt and blight survive in the soil and crop residue. If you are not sure of the cause, show a sample of the affected plant to your local agriculture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra.

## Blossom end rot
Blossom end rot is a dark, sunken, leathery patch at the bottom end of the tomato fruit. It is caused by a lack of calcium reaching the developing fruit, which is most often due to uneven watering rather than a lack of calcium in the soil. Keeping soil moisture steady with regular watering and mulching is the main way to prevent it.
