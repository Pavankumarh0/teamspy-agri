# Soil Health

Review status: starter content, to be checked against the official package of practices.

## What healthy soil means
Healthy soil holds water and air, supplies nutrients to the crop, and is full of living organisms such as earthworms and microbes. Soil organic matter is the key: it feeds soil life, binds soil particles into crumbs, and helps the soil hold both water and nutrients.

## How to improve soil health
Add organic matter regularly, for example well-decomposed farmyard manure, compost, vermicompost or green manure, because organic matter improves soil structure, water holding and nutrient supply. Rotate crops, and include legumes such as pulses, because legumes add nitrogen to the soil and rotation breaks pest and disease cycles. Keep the soil covered with a crop, cover crop or mulch, because bare soil loses moisture and topsoil to sun, wind and rain. Avoid burning crop residue, because burning destroys organic matter and soil organisms; incorporate or compost it instead. Avoid unnecessary deep ploughing and working wet soil, because these damage soil structure and cause compaction.

## Soil testing
A soil test shows the soil pH and the levels of nutrients such as nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium. Fertilizer and lime or gypsum recommendations should be based on a soil test, because applying nutrients the soil already has wastes money and can harm the soil and water. Farmers in India can get their soil tested and receive a Soil Health Card, which lists the nutrient status of the field and fertilizer recommendations for the crop.

## Soil pH
Soil pH affects how well plants can take up nutrients. Acidic soils are usually corrected with agricultural lime, and alkaline or sodic soils with gypsum, but the type and amount must come from a soil test recommendation.
