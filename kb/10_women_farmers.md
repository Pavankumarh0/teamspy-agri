# Women Farmers

Sources: Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare replies in Parliament (Lok Sabha); Ministry of Rural Development (DAY-NRLM); Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005. Review status: starter content, to be checked by the local agriculture office.

## Women farmers make farm decisions
Women farmers do much of the work in Indian agriculture, and a woman farmer can make every farm decision herself, including which crop to grow, which fertilizer to buy and how much to apply. The same advice, soil tests and recommendations apply to every farmer, woman or man. It is not true that women cannot understand fertilizers: fertilizer advice is the same and just as easy to follow for a woman as for a man, so it can be explained directly to the woman farmer herself. Fertilizer choice depends on the crop and the soil, not on who the farmer is. A woman farmer who wants to know her fertilizer dose can get her soil tested, read her Soil Health Card, or ask the local agriculture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra, and use the Smart Krishi fertilizer screen for her crop and land.

## Government support for women farmers
The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare asks states and implementing agencies to spend at least 30 percent of the funds of its beneficiary-oriented schemes and programmes on women farmers. The Mahila Kisan Sashaktikaran Pariyojana (MKSP) is a programme only for women farmers. It is part of the Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana - National Rural Livelihoods Mission of the Ministry of Rural Development and supports women farmers, often through self-help groups, to use sustainable and agro-ecological practices. Women farmers can ask the agriculture office, the Krishi Vigyan Kendra or the local self-help group federation about these schemes.

## Land and benefits in a woman's name
Under the Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005, daughters have the same rights as sons in ancestral property. Land registered in a woman's name lets her apply directly for farm schemes, crop insurance and a Soil Health Card for that land. A woman who has taken over the farm, for example after her husband's death, can update the land records with the revenue office and then apply in her own name.

## Training and help
Krishi Vigyan Kendras in every district train farmers, including women farmers, in crop management, soil testing and new practices. Women farmers can also get soil testing and advice from the Raitha Samparka Kendra and the local agriculture officer.
