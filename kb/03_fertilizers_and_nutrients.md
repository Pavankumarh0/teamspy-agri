# Fertilizers and Plant Nutrients

Review status: starter content, to be checked against the official package of practices.

## Main nutrients and what they do
Nitrogen (N) supports leaf and stem growth and green colour; a shortage shows as pale or yellow older leaves. Phosphorus (P) supports root growth, flowering and fruit setting. Potassium (K) supports fruit quality and size, water regulation and resistance to stress and disease. Crops also need secondary nutrients such as calcium, magnesium and sulphur, and micronutrients such as zinc and boron in small amounts.

## Common fertilizers
Urea supplies nitrogen. DAP (diammonium phosphate) supplies phosphorus and some nitrogen. Muriate of potash (MOP) supplies potassium. NPK complex fertilizers supply all three in fixed proportions. Organic manures such as compost, farmyard manure and vermicompost supply small amounts of many nutrients and also improve the soil.

## How much fertilizer to use
The right fertilizer amount depends on the crop, the variety, the season and the nutrients already in the soil, so it should be based on a soil test. Use the recommendation on your Soil Health Card or from your local agriculture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra. Using more fertilizer than recommended does not increase yield; it wastes money, can burn plants, encourages pests on soft growth, and pollutes soil and water.

## How to apply fertilizer well
Apply phosphorus and potassium mostly at or before planting, because roots need them early. Give nitrogen in split doses over the season instead of all at once, because nitrogen is easily lost by washing away or into the air. Apply fertilizer to moist soil near the root zone and not touching stems or leaves, and water after applying, so nutrients reach the roots and do not burn the plant. Combine chemical fertilizers with organic manure, which is called integrated nutrient management, because organic matter helps the soil hold nutrients and improves long-term soil health.
