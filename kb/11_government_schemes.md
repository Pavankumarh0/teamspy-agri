# Government Schemes for Farmers

Sources: Press Information Bureau (PM-KISAN); Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare. Scheme rules and amounts change: confirm the current details with the agriculture office, the bank or the official scheme portal before applying. Review status: starter content.

## PM-KISAN income support
Under PM-KISAN (Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi), each eligible farmer family gets 6000 rupees a year, paid in three equal instalments of 2000 rupees, directly into an Aadhaar-seeded bank account. eKYC must be completed to receive the instalments. To apply or check the status, farmers can contact the local agriculture office or use the official PM-KISAN portal with their land and bank details.

## Crop insurance (PMFBY)
The Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana is the government's crop insurance scheme. It protects farmers against crop loss from natural causes such as drought, flood, pests and disease. The farmer pays only a small part of the premium and the government pays the rest. Farmers can enrol through their bank, the agriculture office or the official PMFBY portal before the cut-off date for each season.

## Soil Health Card scheme
Under the Soil Health Card scheme, farmers get their soil tested and receive a card that shows the nutrient status of their field and recommends fertilizer for their crops. Contact the agriculture office or the Raitha Samparka Kendra to have soil samples taken.

## Drip and sprinkler irrigation support
The government supports drip and sprinkler irrigation, which save water, under the Per Drop More Crop part of the Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana. The share of the cost that is supported differs by state and by farmer category, so ask the agriculture or horticulture department for the current subsidy before buying a system.

## Where to ask about schemes
The local agriculture officer, the Raitha Samparka Kendra, the Krishi Vigyan Kendra and the bank can explain which schemes a farmer is eligible for and help with the application. Market prices change every day; the kiosk can tell today's mandi prices for common crops, or check the local market (APMC) or the government's agricultural market price portal for today's prices.
