# Soil Moisture and Irrigation

Review status: starter content, to be checked against the official package of practices.

## When to water crops
Water when the soil around the roots starts to dry, not on a fixed habit. Check the soil at root depth, not only the surface: take soil from a few centimetres down and press it in your hand. If it holds together and feels moist, the crop usually does not need water yet; if it is dry and crumbly, it is time to irrigate. The best time to water is early morning or evening, because less water is lost to evaporation and leaves dry before night, which reduces disease.

## How to water
Watering deeply and less often is usually better than frequent light watering, because deep watering encourages deep roots that can find water during dry spells. Water at the base of the plant and avoid wetting the leaves, because wet leaves help fungal diseases spread. Avoid waterlogging, because roots need air; standing water damages roots and can cause yellow leaves and wilting. Water needs change with crop stage, soil type and weather: sandy soil dries faster than clay soil, and crops need more water in hot, windy weather.

## How to improve soil moisture
Mulch the soil with straw, dry leaves, crop residue or plastic mulch, because mulch shades the soil, reduces evaporation and keeps weeds down. Add organic matter such as compost or farmyard manure, because organic matter acts like a sponge and helps soil hold more water. Use drip irrigation where possible, because it delivers water slowly to the root zone with very little waste. Harvest rainwater with farm ponds, bunds and contour trenches, because they slow runoff and let more rain soak into the soil. Reduce unnecessary tillage, because each ploughing exposes moist soil to drying.

## Signs of too little or too much water
Too little water: plants wilt in the afternoon and do not recover by evening, leaves curl or turn dull, and growth slows. Too much water: soil stays soggy, lower leaves turn yellow, roots may rot, and plants wilt even though the soil is wet.
