# Tomato Cultivation

Review status: starter content, to be checked against the official package of practices.

## Soil and field preparation for tomato
Tomato grows best in well-drained, fertile loamy soil rich in organic matter. It does not tolerate waterlogging, so avoid low fields where water stands, or plant on raised beds or ridges. Mix well-decomposed farmyard manure or compost into the soil while preparing the field, because organic matter improves drainage, water holding and nutrient supply for the whole season.

## Fertilizer for tomato
Tomato needs nitrogen for leaf and vine growth, phosphorus for strong roots and flowering, and potassium for fruit size, colour and quality. Calcium is important for healthy fruit; a lack of calcium reaching the fruit causes blossom end rot. A common practice is to apply organic manure and the phosphorus and potassium at transplanting, and to give nitrogen in split doses during growth and fruiting. The exact fertilizer quantities depend on your soil test, so follow your Soil Health Card or the recommendation of your local agriculture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra. Too much nitrogen gives lots of leaves but fewer fruits and makes plants softer and more attractive to pests.

## Watering tomato
Tomato needs steady soil moisture, especially during flowering and fruit development. Irregular watering, with dry spells followed by heavy watering, causes fruit cracking and blossom end rot. Drip irrigation and mulching help keep moisture even. Water at the base of the plant and avoid wetting the leaves, because wet leaves spread diseases such as early blight.

## Staking and care
Staking or trellising tomato plants keeps fruit and leaves off the soil, which reduces fruit rot and leaf disease and makes spraying and harvesting easier. Remove badly diseased lower leaves and keep the field free of weeds, which compete for water and nutrients and can harbour pests.
