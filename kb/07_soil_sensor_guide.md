# Soil Moisture Sensor Guide

Review status: starter content, to be checked against the official package of practices.

## What the soil moisture reading means
The Smart Krishi soil sensor reports soil moisture as a percentage. This percentage is a relative reading from the sensor, not a laboratory measurement, and the same number can mean different things in sandy soil and in clay soil. A higher number means wetter soil and a lower number means drier soil.

## How to use the reading to decide watering
Learn what the readings mean in your own field. Note the reading the day after a thorough watering, when the soil is well moist, and note the reading when the soil at root depth feels dry and the crop needs water. Use these two readings from your own field as your guide: when the reading falls close to your dry reading, it is time to water. Always confirm by checking the soil by hand at root depth before irrigating, especially when the sensor is new.

## Uncalibrated soil sensor
Until the soil sensor has been calibrated in your own field, Smart Krishi shows its raw reading only and does not give a soil moisture percentage, because a raw number from an uncalibrated sensor cannot tell you how wet the soil is. A raw reading stuck at the very top of the scale usually means the sensor is not touching moist soil, is disconnected or is wired wrongly, so it should be checked before it is trusted.

## Air quality sensor
The MQ-135 air quality sensor gives a raw value that changes with gases such as ammonia and carbon dioxide, and also with temperature and humidity. Without calibration against a reference instrument it does not measure a gas level, ppm or an air quality index.
