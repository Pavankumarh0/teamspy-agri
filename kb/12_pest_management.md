# Pest and Disease Management

Review status: starter content on integrated pest management (IPM), to be checked against the state agricultural university's package of practices. This information does not give pesticide names or doses: for those, follow the advice of the agriculture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra and the product label.

## Integrated pest management
Integrated pest management means preventing pests first and using chemicals only when needed. Grow healthy crops with balanced fertilizer, rotate crops, remove and destroy infected plants and crop residue, and keep fields free of weeds that shelter pests. Check the crop regularly and use traps to see how many pests there are. Protect natural enemies such as ladybird beetles and spiders, because they eat many pests. Use a pesticide only when the pest damage is above the level experts advise, and choose the product recommended for that pest.

## Pheromone traps and sticky traps
Pheromone traps attract and catch male moths, which shows when moth pests such as the cotton pink bollworm, tomato fruit borer and fall armyworm arrive and reduces their mating. Yellow sticky traps catch small flying insects such as whiteflies and aphids, which helps to watch and reduce them. Ask the Krishi Vigyan Kendra for the right trap and how many to place.

## Cotton pink bollworm
Pink bollworm larvae feed inside cotton bolls, so damage is hard to see from outside. Pheromone traps help to watch the moths. Destroying cotton stalks and crop residue after harvest, and not keeping the crop in the field longer than needed, breaks the pest's life cycle. Rosette (twisted) flowers and bored bolls are signs of attack; remove and destroy them.

## Fall armyworm in maize
Fall armyworm caterpillars eat maize leaves and the whorl, leaving ragged holes and sawdust-like droppings in the whorl. Check young maize plants often, use pheromone traps, and remove and destroy egg masses and young caterpillars by hand when found. Early detection matters, because older caterpillars are harder to control.

## Aphids on chilli and other crops
Aphids are small soft insects that suck sap from young leaves and spread viruses. Yellow sticky traps, removing badly infested shoots and protecting natural enemies such as ladybird beetles help to keep them down. Neem-based products are commonly used against aphids; follow the label.

## Potato late blight
Late blight is a fungal disease of potato and tomato that spreads fast in cool, humid and rainy weather. It causes dark, water-soaked patches on leaves that spread quickly, with white growth on the underside in moist conditions, and it can rot the tubers. Use healthy seed tubers, grow resistant varieties where available, remove and destroy infected plants, avoid overhead watering, and rotate crops. When the weather favours the disease, ask the agriculture officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra about protective sprays.

## Rice stem borer
Stem borer larvae bore into rice stems. Young plants show dead central shoots, called dead hearts, and older plants show white, empty panicles, called white ears. Remove and destroy dead hearts, use pheromone traps, and avoid excess nitrogen, because soft lush growth attracts more pests.
